The Magic of Software Engineering

“The magic of myth and legend has come true in our time. One types the correct incantation on a keyboard, and a display screen comes to life, showing things that never were nor could be.... ”

— Fred Brooks

"Magic" is a term used to describe a process whose details are so well hidden that it seems impossible. To me, software engineering at its finest, is exactly that.

As a software engineer, my concern is with managing complexity while keeping it out of view from the audience. Knowledge, entertainment, business, and so much more, all accessed on-demand by way of software, and users need not spare a thought to the details of how it's all done.

But to me, thinking about and exploring those very details are an endless source of curiosity and fascination.

From data structures and algorithms, to design patterns and code architecture, to the technologies and distributed systems that power data-intensive applications, and now to AI models which are redefining the entire field, there is always more to learn in the production of good software.

Code demands clarity, clarity requires care

“The only way to go fast, is to go well.”

— Robert C. Martin

Human life is constantly evolving, and so must software with it. The construction of software has always demanded clarity of intent to distill requirements from vague and complex realities, precision to describe logic and correctness, and sound judgement to make the right tradeoffs in developing sustainable software.

In the age of AI coding models, code has become drastically easier to produce. But 10x the code does not automatically translate to 10x the value. Clarity, precision, and sound judgement are crucial to unlocking the true value of the advantages that AI provides, while keeping codebases from spiraling out of control.

Regardless of the tools I use, my personal principles have always stayed the same - any code I touch should end up in a better state than when I found it.

From Concept to Execution

Project Experience

Always on the lookout for better solutions, I have a proven track record of success in delivering and maintaining software that makes a difference.

Employee Health & Benefits Insurance Platform

In my current role at Marsh, I develop and maintain insurance platforms tailored for specific market geographies - serving business teams and tens of thousands of employees and their dependents.

As a critical and longstanding driver of business revenue and customer enablement, the platform is constantly evolving, providing features such as complex policy administration, claims processing, survey-based enrollment management, and more.

Insurance Policy Administration System

During my tenure at Guidewire, I worked on a policy administration system (Guidewire PolicyCenter) acting as the central business cockpit for one of the largest reinsurers in the world.

The system utilises a graph structure to represent complex policy contract lifecycles, and includes multiple business-critical integrations such as SAP, Solr, Okta etc.

Framework for Synthetic Face Blending

As part of a research project during my internship at UsideU in Tokyo, I developed a framework using a PyTorch port of Nvidia's StyleGAN to generate blends of faces.

With the framework, a multilayer perceptron encoder can be trained to map one-hot encoded vectors that represent selected target faces to a vector (512 dimensional) in the latent input space of StyleGAN's synthesis network.

Traffic Simulator + Cooperative Lane-Changing Driving Scheme

For my final-year project in university, I built a traffic simulator from scratch in MATLAB and designed an autonomous cooperative driving scheme for efficient lane-changing.

Smart City Model

While working as a research assistant during my university studies, I constructed a scaled-down model of an intelligent transport system in 2 months.

I programmed line-following robots in C++, a vehicle positioning camera program in Python, and an IoT system for inter-vehicular communication to navigate routes and avoid collisions with each other.
